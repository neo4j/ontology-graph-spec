= Interoperability
:description: How RDFS and SHACL constructs map onto a Neo4j Ontology Graph Specification model, what the database enforces, and what falls short.

The format covers the parts of RDFS and SHACL that describe a domain, not all of either. It is not RDF, not OWL: no
triples, no IRIs, no inference. It is descriptive first: a model says what a domain looks like. Only part of what it
says is enforced in the database.

The coverage tables name fields from the xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[],
xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[], xref:format:reference/constraints-and-indexes.adoc[] and
xref:format:reference/extensions.adoc[] pages. Constructs with no native field travel in custom data. Each row
gets one of these verdicts:

* *covered*: maps to native fields.
* *partially covered*: maps, with limits.
* *lossy*: maps, but drops meaning.
* *carried*: no native field. Travels in custom data.
* *different mechanism*: met another way.
* *excluded*: deliberately out.
* *n/a*: not a construct of that vocabulary.

== RDFS coverage

[cols="2,4,1"]
|===
| Construct | Mapping | Verdict

| `rdfs:Class` | A xref:format:reference/nodes.adoc[node]. Its `labels` carry the class: `identifier` names it. `implied` and
`optional` add further labels. The `label` shorthand expands into `labels` when the model is decoded. | covered

| `rdf:Property` | A xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[relationship] when its range is a class, a
xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[property] when its range is a datatype. Nothing records which it came from: a
tool importing RDF decides. A property with several domains or ranges becomes one relationship entry per pair of
ends. RDFS joins them conjunctively. The per-pair entries do not carry that: the combination survives as the
identifier and `implied` labels of one node. | covered

| `rdfs:domain` / `rdfs:range` | The `from` and `to` ends of a xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[relationship]. A
property's range via its xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[type]. Types are Neo4j types, not xsd datatypes: a tool
importing RDF maps them. | covered

| `rdfs:label` | `aliases` on a xref:format:reference/nodes.adoc[node],
xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[relationship] or xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[property]. A property
also has `name` (its name in the database when different from its id). A node has no name field. | covered

| `rdfs:comment` | `description` on the model, a node, a relationship or a property: one string each, capped as the
xref:format:rules.adoc[length rules] set out. | covered

| `rdfs:seeAlso`, `rdfs:isDefinedBy` | `reference` on a xref:format:reference/nodes.adoc[node],
xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[relationship] or xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[property]: one URI
each. Further values ride xref:format:reference/extensions.adoc[custom data]. | partially covered

| `rdfs:subClassOf` | No hierarchy to traverse: a tool importing RDF flattens it into the `implied` labels of a
xref:format:reference/nodes.adoc[node]. `implied` and `optional` are plain labels. Nothing infers over them. | covered

| `rdfs:subPropertyOf` | No field on a node, relationship or property. Property hierarchies are excluded. | excluded

| containers (`Bag`/`Seq`/`Alt`), reification | No field. The list xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[types], such as
`LIST<STRING>`, are ordered lists, not RDF containers. Reification has no carrier. | excluded

| cardinality, enums, patterns | Not RDFS constructs, but native here: xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[count],
xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[min_count] and xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[max_count] on a
relationship end. xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[one_of] and xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[pattern] on
a property. | n/a
|===

== SHACL coverage

[cols="2,4,1"]
|===
| Construct | Mapping | Verdict

| `sh:datatype` | The xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[type] of a property, a Neo4j type rather than an xsd
datatype. `VECTOR` types pair with `dimension`, and `ANY` opts the property out. Enforceable as a `PROPERTY_TYPE`
xref:format:reference/constraints-and-indexes.adoc[constraint]. | covered

| `sh:in` | xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[one_of] on a property of a node or relationship: a list of strings, so
non-string literals must be quoted. Mutually exclusive with `pattern`. SHACL allows both together. |
partially covered

| `sh:pattern` | xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[pattern] on a property: a single regex string, with flags such
as `(?i)` folded into it. Mutually exclusive with `one_of`. Descriptive, not enforced. | covered

| `sh:minCount` = 1 on property | xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[must_exist] on a property: shorthand for an
`EXISTS` constraint, expanded when the model is decoded. Enforceable via the database schema. A minimum above 1 has
no native field. | covered

| `sh:minCount`/`sh:maxCount` on relationship | xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[count] alone, or
xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[min_count] with xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[max_count], on a
relationship end. Descriptive: not yet enforced in the database. | covered

| `sh:maxCount` > 1 on property | A list xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[type], such as `LIST<STRING>`, or
`LIST<ANY>` for mixed elements. Gives a list, not a bound on length. Element types are always scalars. | lossy

| `sh:class` | The `node` or `label` of a xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[relationship end], pointing at a
xref:format:reference/nodes.adoc[node] whose `identifier` label is the class. A relationship is its type and two ends:
the same type between other nodes is a separate entry. | covered

| `sh:nodeKind` | The shape of the model itself: literals live under `properties`, node references only as relationship
ends. IRIs collapse into relationships and labels, literals into property types. Nothing distinguishes an IRI from a
blank node: blank nodes have no carrier. | lossy

| `sh:name`, `sh:description` | `description` on a node, relationship or property. The only `name` fields are a
property's (its name in the database when different from its id) and the model's own. No display name for a node or
relationship. | lossy

| `sh:message`, `sh:severity` | No native field. Rides xref:format:reference/extensions.adoc[custom data]. The format
has no severity concept. | lossy

| `sh:minInclusive/maxExclusive` etc. (value ranges) | No native field on a property: only `pattern` and
`one_of` restrict values. Rides xref:format:reference/extensions.adoc[custom data]. See <<_gaps_and_caveats>>. |
carried

| `sh:minLength/maxLength` | No native field on a property. Rides xref:format:reference/extensions.adoc[custom
data]. Not the length caps on the xref:format:rules.adoc[rules] page: those bound the model's own `name` and
`description`, not property values. See <<_gaps_and_caveats>>. | carried

| `sh:languageIn`, `sh:uniqueLang` | No native field. Rides xref:format:reference/extensions.adoc[custom data].
Property values carry no language tags. | carried

| `sh:equals`, `sh:disjoint`, `sh:lessThan` (property-pair comparisons) | No native field. Rides
xref:format:reference/extensions.adoc[custom data]. See <<_gaps_and_caveats>>. | carried

| `sh:hasValue` | No dedicated field. Rides xref:format:reference/extensions.adoc[custom data]. Approximate it with
`one_of` plus `must_exist`: a single value, strings only, and it forces presence. | carried

| `sh:closed`, `sh:ignoredProperties` | No native field. Rides xref:format:reference/extensions.adoc[custom data].
Neo4j is schema-optional: a closed world is enforcement policy, not ontology content. | carried

| `sh:order`, `sh:group` | No native field. Rides xref:format:reference/extensions.adoc[custom data]. The
nearest native field to styling is a node's `display`. | carried

| property paths (inverse) | No field of its own: the `from` and `to` ends of a
xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[relationship] entry express them by direction. | different mechanism

| property paths (alternative/sequence) | Not constraints in this format. Anything beyond a single hop rides
xref:format:reference/extensions.adoc[custom data]. | carried

| `sh:not/and/or/xone`, qualified cardinality | No boolean composition: the
xref:format:reference/constraints-and-indexes.adoc[constraint types] are only `EXISTS`, `UNIQUE`, `KEY` and
`PROPERTY_TYPE`. | excluded

| SHACL-SPARQL constraints | No SPARQL machinery. Raw text at best in xref:format:reference/extensions.adoc[custom
data]. | excluded

| SHACL-AF rules/functions | No field on the model, deliberately: rules and functions imply inference, which the
format does not do. | excluded
|===

== Enforcement

A model is descriptive first. The xref:libraries:validation.adoc[libraries] validate the model document itself, not
the data in the database. The database enforces only what its schema can carry.

Enforced in the database:

* The `identifier` label of a node and the `type` of a relationship: they select what the constraints apply to. The
`implied` and `optional` labels are descriptive.
* xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[must_exist], `unique` and `key` on a property, and the
xref:format:reference/constraints-and-indexes.adoc[constraints] of a node or relationship: the four constraint types,
`EXISTS`, `UNIQUE`, `KEY` and `PROPERTY_TYPE`.

Descriptive, not enforced:

* The `from` and `to` ends of a relationship and their xref:format:reference/relationships.adoc[count], `min_count`
and `max_count`.
* xref:format:reference/properties.adoc[one_of] and `pattern` on a property.
* `name`, `description`, `aliases`, `reference` and `dimension`.
* `extensions`, and the custom data in them.

== Gaps and caveats

Gaps, likeliest to matter first:

* Value ranges: `sh:minInclusive`, `sh:maxExclusive` and the rest. Plausible in regulated industries (ingredient
classes, dosage ranges). No native field on a property. The route is an owner-defined validation object in
xref:format:reference/extensions.adoc[custom data].
* String lengths: `sh:minLength` and `sh:maxLength`. Cheap to add on the same validation object. Nothing on a
property today. Not the length caps on the xref:format:rules.adoc[rules] page: those bound the model's own `name` and
`description`, not property values.
* Property-pair comparisons: `sh:equals`, `sh:disjoint`, `sh:lessThan`. Rare in practice. The same route through
custom data.

Caveats:

* SHACL validates instance data after loading it. This format is descriptive first, enforced via the database schema
where possible. Validation checks the model document, not the data.
* SHACL binds shapes to data with targets such as `sh:targetClass`. Here the binding is identity: a
xref:format:reference/nodes.adoc[node] entry is the shape for its `identifier` label. No target machinery.
